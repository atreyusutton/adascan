# Honest risks

Ordered by how much they matter, not by how comfortable they are.

## 1. Automated remediation genuinely is not solved

`[G]` DOJ's stated reason for the deadline extension was the limits of generative AI for
remediation. **That is simultaneously the reason this opportunity exists and the biggest
warning attached to it.**

Complex tables, scanned forms, maps and multi-column layouts still need a person.

**Mitigation:** do not promise full automation. Price a **hybrid** and be explicit about
what gets human review. Overpromising here is how you end up certifying something
non-compliant and inheriting the customer's liability.

## 2. Liability transfer

If you tell a city they're compliant and they get sued, that's partly your problem.

**Mitigation, before signing anything:**
- Get a lawyer to write the engagement terms
- Get **errors and omissions coverage**

**Do not skip this.** It is a prerequisite to the first paid engagement, not a later
cleanup item.

Supporting posture: a human signs off on every batch
([`human-in-the-loop.md`](human-in-the-loop.md)), and the audit log records who approved
what ([`architecture.md`](architecture.md), Stage 6).

## 3. Procurement is slow

May require an **RFP**. Mid-size entities take 6–12 months to close.

**Mitigation:** start with **small districts and school boards** that can buy under a
direct-purchase threshold. Do not build year one on mid-size entities.

Related: `[E]` government budget calendars mean a district that wants to buy in March
often has no money until January. Sales cycle is dictated by their budget cycle, not
your effort. See [`economics.md`](economics.md).

## 4. Incumbents

`[G]` **Allyant, Equidox, CommonLook, Level Access**, plus a wave of AI entrants.

**You do not beat them on technology.** You beat them on:
- **Price**
- **Being the person who showed a school district a number nobody had shown them**

### And stay far away from overlay widget vendors

`[G]` Overlay vendors have a genuinely bad reputation and get sued over it. **Do real
remediation.** This is a positioning rule, not a preference — proximity to that category
is itself a liability.

## 5. The deadline could move again

It has already moved once.

**Mitigation:** `[G]` Colorado's **HB21-1110** doesn't depend on DOJ — its deadline has
already passed and it carries a $3,500-per-violation penalty payable to plaintiffs. That
is one more reason to start in Colorado: state demand survives a federal slip.

## 6. The window closes either way

`[E]` Even if the deadlines hold, urgency collapses after April 2028. Roughly **24 months
at peak intensity.**

This is a "make a lot of money in two years" business, not a compounding one — *unless*
the customer base is converted into something else before the window closes. What that
something else is has not been answered. See [`open-questions.md`](open-questions.md).

## 7. The prospect count is softer than it looks

`[E]` A large share of Colorado's 2,808 special districts are shell metro districts with
a five-page website and no documents. Assume 20–30% are real prospects — 600 to 800.
Any plan built on the raw count is built on sand.
